# The Fabric of the Cosmos: An Analysis of Relativity

The theory of relativity revolutionized our understanding of space and time. Before Einstein, physics assumed that time and space were absolute and fixed. Einstein challenged this, proposing that time and space are relative and that they vary depending on the observer. This shift in perspective is what we call the theory of relativity. The theory is divided into two parts: Special Relativity and General Relativity.

### 1. Special Relativity
Special relativity is the first part of the theory. It deals with the relationship between space and time. The theory's main assumption is that the laws of physics are the same regardless of the motion of the observer. The two fundamental pillars of special relativity are: 
1. The speed of light is constant.
2. The speed of light is the same for all observers.

These lead to the concept of time dilation and length contraction. The theory’s effect is that time and distance change depending on the observer. This means that if you move fast enough, time slows down and distance shrinks. This is called the relativity of time and space.

### 2. General Relativity
General relativity is the second part of the theory. It extends the special theory by including gravity. The theory’s core idea is that space-time warps when a massive object is present. This is called the curvature of space-time. The theory’s theory is that gravity is not a force, but a consequence of the fact that the space-time is curved. 

The theory’s prediction is that particles of light cannot travel in a straight line; they follow a curved path. The theory’s consequence is that the bending of light is a direct effect of the curvature of space-time. This is called the gravitational lens effect.

### Summary
The theory’s main point is that the universe is not flat but curved. The theory’s core idea is that gravity is the result of the curvature of space-time. The theory’s theory is that the curvature of space-time is a direct effect of the mass inside. This is called the theory of relativity.
